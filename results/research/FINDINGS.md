# Deep findings — distribution-free sequential learning on Jane Street

Research notes (not paper prose). Generated by `scripts/research_findings.py`,
`scripts/research_fairness.py`, `scripts/research_windows.py`. Numbers are on the
standardized Jane Street slice `date_id ∈ [0,30)`, target `responder_6`, weighted
zero-mean R² metric, 100k–200k rows, unless stated otherwise.

These probes pressure-test the four headline claims from the first-pass
experiments. **Three of the four needed substantial revision.** The corrected
story is stronger and more defensible, but different.

---

## Finding 1 — OGD's "R² = −286 catastrophe" is a learning-rate artifact (RQ1)

The first-pass benchmark ran every method at the shared `lr = 5e-3` from
`configs/jane.yaml` — a rate tuned for the *clipped* methods. At that rate OGD
diverges (R² = −286). Swept over its own learning rate, OGD is fine: its best is
**R² = +0.028 at lr = 2e-3**, and it is positive for all `lr ≤ 2e-3`.

**Do not** claim "OGD explodes to −286": that is a cherry-picked lr and a reviewer
will catch it. Use the fair framing in Findings 2 and 5.

## Finding 2 — Clipping's value is a ~15× larger *stable* learning rate (RQ1, RQ1b)

- At any fixed lr in OGD's stable range (`≤ 2e-3`), clipping is neutral-to-
  slightly-harmful — it only discards useful gradient signal. Clipping is a
  *stability* mechanism, not a per-step accuracy mechanism.
- OGD is stable to `lr ≈ 2e-3`; the best clippers stay stable to `lr ≈ 3e-2`
  (**~15× wider**). Because more predictive signal is available at a larger step
  size, that extended range is where the real gains live.

## Finding 3 — The intrinsic gradient tail is α ≈ 2.43, not 10¹⁴ (RQ2)

The old motivation figure showed gradient norms up to 10¹⁴. Those came from OGD's
*own diverging trajectory* — a circular artifact. Measured at the **fixed** best
predictor w* (learner-independent), the gradient norm distribution is:

- **Hill tail index α ≈ 2.43** → finite variance, **infinite fourth moment**.
- excess kurtosis ≈ 111, p99.9/mean ≈ 19×, max ≈ 1149 vs mean ≈ 17.
- At w = 0 (the learner's start) the tail is essentially identical (α ≈ 2.40).

Real gradients live exactly in the `2 < α < 4` regime that finite-second-moment
theory targets and finite-fourth-moment clipped-SGD analyses do not cover.
**Replace the 10¹⁴ figure with this** (raw sample: `gradnorm_at_wstar.npy`).

## Finding 4 — The heavy gradient tail is an interaction effect: feature × residual tail dependence (RQ3)

Decomposing g = 2·ω·(x·w*−y)·x into its factors (Hill α, learner-independent):

| quantity | Hill α | excess kurtosis | note |
|----------|--------|-----------------|------|
| gradient norm ‖g‖ | **2.43** | 111 | infinite 4th moment |
| residual \|x·w*−y\| | 4.45 | 5.9 | finite 4th moment |
| feature norm ‖x‖ | 4.08 | 164 | finite 4th moment, spiky |
| sample weight ω | 31.8 | 1.0 | ~light-tailed |
| target \|y\| | 4.36 | 6.0 | clipped to ±5 |

Each factor individually has α ≳ 4 (finite fourth moment). For a product of
*independent* regularly-varying factors the tail index is min(αᵢ) ≈ 4.08 — yet
the gradient's α ≈ 2.43 is **heavier than any factor**. The only way a product
tail is heavier than its lightest factor is **positive tail dependence**: extreme
features and extreme residuals hit on the *same* rounds (joint microstructure
shocks). The heavy gradient tail is *manufactured by co-movement*, not inherited
from features or noise alone. Novel, mechanistic, and a good motivation hook.

## Finding 5 — Fair ranking overturns "Catoni is best"; Catoni is in fact the *worst* clipper (RQ1b)

Per-method lr-tuned best weighted R² (grid `{1e-3 … 4e-2}`, 100k rows):

| method | best R² | @ lr | breaks down at |
|--------|---------|------|----------------|
| RobustOMD (median-of-means) | **0.1487** | 3e-2 | 4e-2 |
| AdaptiveClip (quantile) | 0.1377 | 3e-2 | 4e-2 |
| RobustOMD (trimmed mean) | 0.1280 | 3e-2 | 4e-2 |
| RobustOMD (Catoni) | 0.0786 | 2e-2 | **3e-2** |
| OGD | 0.0285 | 2e-3 | 5e-3 |

- **Best clipper (MoM) beats OGD by 5.2×** in weighted R² (0.149 vs 0.028).
- **Catoni is the worst of the four clippers**, the exact opposite of the shared-lr
  benchmark's claim. It peaks at 0.079 and *diverges at 3e-2* (R² = −0.21) where
  the others are near their best (0.13–0.15).
- **Mechanism:** Catoni's soft log-truncation influence ψ(t)=sign(t)·log(1+|t|+t²/2)
  is unbounded (grows, slowly, forever), so it never *fully* rejects an extreme
  gradient; at large lr the surviving outliers destabilise it. MoM and the trimmed
  mean *hard-reject* whole blocks / order statistics, giving a wider stable-lr
  margin. **For pushing the step size — which is where clipping pays — prefer
  hard-rejection estimators over soft truncation.**
- **Why the benchmark got it wrong:** at the shared conservative lr = 5e-3 every
  clipper sits in its linear regime and is indistinguishable (0.042–0.048); the
  methods only separate at high lr. Evaluating at one conservative lr hides all
  the structure. **Report an R²-vs-lr curve per method, not a single-lr table.**

## Finding 6 — Standardization and clipping are complements, not substitutes (RQ5)

| | OGD | AdaptiveClip | Catoni |
|--|-----|--------------|--------|
| standardized (lr 1e-3) | 0.0234 | 0.0180 | 0.0198 |
| raw features (lr 1e-6) | 0.0038 | 0.0008 | 0.0012 |

On raw (unstandardized) features, everything collapses to R² ≈ 0.001–0.004 even at
a tiny safe lr, and **clipping does not rescue it** (clipped ≈ OGD). Raw features
are *ill-conditioned* (scales differ by 10³), a problem clipping does not address.
Standardization fixes conditioning; clipping fixes heavy tails. You need both.
(Caveat: raw case used a single conservative lr; a per-feature-scaled lr might do
better, but the qualitative conclusion — clipping ≠ substitute for standardization
— is clear.)

## Finding 7 — The winning operating point does not transfer across market regimes (RQ4)

Taking each method's in-sample best lr and applying it to five disjoint date
windows [0,20), [30,50), [100,120), [400,420), [900,920):

- At the in-sample-optimal lr (3e-2 for clippers), the clipped methods **diverge
  catastrophically on 4 of 5 windows** (R² down to −10¹⁴), while OGD at its
  conservative lr (2e-3) is stable and positive on every window
  (mean R² +0.031, worst −0.022).
- Backing the clipped lr down to 1e-2 still diverges on 2 of 5 windows
  ([30,50), [400,420)); backing down to 5e-3 still diverges on 1 of 5 ([30,50)).
- Where the clippers *are* stable they beat OGD by ~1.4–2.4× (e.g. window
  [100,120): Catoni 0.105 vs OGD 0.044). But the win comes with **catastrophic
  tail risk under a fixed lr**.

**Mechanism:** RobustOMD's threshold τ = c·robust_mean(recent ‖g‖) is *adaptive to
the local gradient scale*. In a turbulent regime the whole scale rises, so τ rises
too — clipping guards against spikes *relative to the current scale* but not
against a regime-wide scale increase. With a fixed lr it then diverges much like
OGD, just at a higher lr threshold.

**Caveat (why Finding 8 is needed):** the per-window restart is pessimistic for
the clippers — it resets the lr/sqrt(t) schedule and the 10-sample clipping
warm-up on every window, so each turbulent window is hit with a large effective
step and an unprotected transient. The continuous-stream test (RQ4b) removes this
confound.

## Finding 8 — Deployed continuously, clipping's advantage is real and decisive (RQ4b)

Streaming ONE learner through date[0,120) (300k rows, containing the turbulent
date[30,50) patch), with t growing across the whole span:

| base lr | OGD | AdaptiveClip | MoM | Catoni | Trimmed |
|---------|-----|--------------|-----|--------|---------|
| 2e-3 | −0.065 | 0.028 | 0.029 | 0.029 | 0.027 |
| 5e-3 | **−442** 💥 | 0.046 | 0.050 | 0.050 | 0.045 |
| 1e-2 | **−98,459** 💥 | 0.072 | 0.078 | 0.078 | 0.070 |
| 2e-2 | **−4.9×10⁶** 💥 | 0.114 | 0.123 | 0.097 | 0.111 |
| 3e-2 | **−1.3×10⁹** 💥 | 0.144 | **0.155** | 0.076 | 0.141 |

(peak rolling loss for every clipped cell is ≈ 4.3–5, i.e. bounded; OGD's peak
rolling loss explodes to 8×10⁴ … 2.7×10¹¹.)

- **OGD has no usable learning rate:** small lr underfits (−0.065 at 2e-3), and
  any lr ≥ 5e-3 diverges when the stream hits the turbulent regime. OGD never
  reaches positive weighted R² at any rate on this multi-regime stream.
- **Every clipped method stays bounded across the whole lr range** and R² grows
  monotonically to ≈ 0.155 (median-of-means at 3e-2).
- **This resolves the Finding 7 "fragility":** the per-window divergence was a
  restart artifact (cold clipping + reset lr/sqrt(t)). Streamed continuously, the
  turbulent regime is met with a decayed effective step *and* a warm clipper, so
  it stays bounded. OGD has the *same* decay and still diverges — so the decay is
  not the differentiator, **the clipping is.**
- **Catoni is weakest again** (0.076 @ 3e-2; peak loss 16.9 signals incipient
  instability), consistent with Finding 5.

This is the deployable, honest version of the paper's central claim, and it is
strong: not "OGD explodes to −286 (at a cherry-picked lr)" but **"OGD has no
usable learning rate on heavy-tailed multi-regime streams, whereas distribution-
free clipping keeps the iterate bounded across regimes, enabling a ~15× larger
step and positive R² (up to 0.155) where OGD's best is −0.065."**

## Finding 9 — The heavy tail is doubly nonstationary: both scale AND tail index drift, and a scalar adaptive threshold cannot win (RQ6)

Measured on a contiguous 60-day span (date[0,60), 540k rows), all at the fixed w*
(learner-independent). See `scripts/research_nonstationarity.py`,
`nonstationarity_daily.csv`, `fig_scale_drift.png`, `fig_threshold_tension.png`.

**Scale drift.** Daily median ‖g‖ swings **5.9×** (4.3 → 25.5) across the two
months, with day-over-day jumps up to **3.15× overnight** (mean daily change
≈ 29%). The p99 swings ~10× (40 → 400+).

**Tail-index drift (the sharper finding).** Daily Hill $\hat\alpha$ wanders over
**1.17 – 3.31** and dips **below 2 on several days** — i.e. the gradient becomes
*infinite-variance* intermittently. So the operative moment index $p_t$ is itself
nonstationary and sometimes $p_t < 2$. The "finite variance ($p=2$)" assumption
underpinning even the strongest heavy-tailed theory is *violated on some days*.

**Why a scalar adaptive threshold cannot win.** For a causal trailing threshold
$\tau_t = 3\cdot\mathrm{median}(\|g\|, W)$:

| window $W$ | $\tau$ dynamic range (p95/p5) | clip rate | shock p99.9 ($\|g\|/\tau$) |
|-----------:|:----:|:----:|:----:|
| 250 | 5.95× | 0.149 | 9.4 |
| 1000 | 4.31× | 0.158 | 9.9 |
| 4000 | 2.97× | 0.168 | 10.9 |
| 16000 | 2.10× | 0.171 | 12.1 |

- **Small $W$ (fast):** $\tau$ faithfully tracks the 5.9× scale drift (dynamic
  range 5.95×). But the clipped step is $\propto \tau_t$, so it *inherits* that
  ~6× swing → the fixed-lr divergence of Finding 7.
- **Large $W$ (slow):** $\tau$ is smooth (range 2.1×) so the step is stable, but
  it **lags** regime onsets — larger shocks slip past a stale threshold
  (shock p99.9 rises 9.4 → 12.1) and it over-clips once turbulence arrives.
- **No $W$ escapes both.** A scalar rolling-scale threshold with a fixed
  multiplier cannot simultaneously (i) keep the step bounded across a 6× scale
  drift and (ii) react to a 3×-overnight jump while $\alpha$ dips below 2.

**Algorithmic implication.** The fix is to *decouple the step size from the tail
scale* rather than clip to it — e.g. **normalize** the gradient by a tracked
robust scale ($g_t / s_t$) so the step is scale-invariant, and/or run a
**two-timescale** scheme (fast scale tracker + slow step). This is the concrete
design lead the open problem (PROBLEM.md §5) points to.

## Finding 10 — A scale-normalized step attains the wall: it never diverges AND is the most accurate (RQ7, achievability)

The theory (THEORY.md §4) predicted the fix for the drifting-scale divergence is to
*normalize* the gradient by a tracked scale `s_t` (scale-invariant step `g_t/s_t` +
a **constant, scale-free** cap `M`) rather than *clip* to a scale-dependent `τ_t=c·s_t`
— the two differ by a factor `1/s_t` that removes the scale-drift transmission. We
implemented **SN-OGD** (robustified-EMA `s_t`, `β=0.99`, cap `M=10`, all untuned) and
tested it where clipping failed. `scripts/research_normalize.py`, `fig_normalize.png`,
`normalize_continuous.csv`, `normalize_windows.csv`.

**Continuous multi-regime stream (date[0,120), 300k):** best weighted `R²` at each
method's own best learning rate —

| method | best `R²` | stable lr range | diverges above |
|--------|:---------:|:---------------:|:--------------:|
| OGD | −0.065 | ≤ 2e-3 only | 5e-3 |
| AdaptiveClip | 0.144 | up to 3e-2 | 5e-2 |
| RobustOMD (MoM) | 0.155 | up to 3e-2 | 5e-2 |
| **SN-OGD (ours)** | **0.242** (lr 1.0) | **2e-3 … 2.0, never diverges** | — |

SN-OGD's peak rolling loss stays `≈ 5` across the *entire* range `[2e-3, 2.0]` (others
explode to `10¹²–10¹⁴⁵`), and its accuracy `climbs monotonically` to **0.24–0.26**,
well above the best clipper's 0.155. Scale-invariance helps *both* axes: the step is
well-conditioned in every regime, so it is both un-divergeable and more accurate.

**Per-window restart (where clipping diverged, Finding 7), SN-OGD at lr 0.5:**
all five windows **positive** (mean `R² = 0.186`, 100% bounded), including the
turbulent `[30,50)` at `+0.131` — where OGD hit `−2.9×10¹⁰` and AdaptiveClip `−444`.
Even mis-tuned high (lr 1.0) SN-OGD only degrades *gracefully* (mildly negative,
bounded peak ≤ 21), never catastrophically.

| setting | OGD survived | AdaptiveClip survived | **SN-OGD survived** |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 10 per-window restarts | 50% | 70% | **100%** |

**This is the matching achievability story to the §3 lower-bound wall:** near-`√T`
dynamic regret is impossible, but the *right* algorithm — scale-normalized, not
scale-clipped — stays bounded and attains strong performance across all regimes
*without per-regime tuning*. It attains the worst-tail rate instead of diverging.

*Caveats [honest]:* SN-OGD's best lr differs by setting (continuous `~1.0`, restart
`~0.5`), so it is not fully tuning-free — but its stable range is ~1000× wider than
clipping's and it degrades gracefully. Hyperparameters `(β, M, winsor)` are untuned
defaults. This is *empirical* achievability; a proven high-probability *dynamic*
regret bound for SN-OGD under drifting `(σ_t,p_t)` remains open (THEORY §4, T-ii).

## Net effect on the paper's story

- The heavy-tail **problem** is real and measured honestly: intrinsic gradient
  α ≈ 2.43, driven by feature–residual tail dependence (Findings 3–4).
- The **central claim**, deployed on a continuous multi-regime stream (Finding 8):
  **plain OGD has no usable learning rate — small rates underfit, larger rates
  diverge on turbulent regimes; distribution-free clipping keeps the iterate
  bounded across regimes, enabling a ~15× larger step (2e-3 → 3e-2) and positive
  weighted R² up to 0.155, where OGD's best is −0.065.** This replaces the
  unfair "OGD → −286 at a shared lr" framing.
- The **estimator story** flips (Findings 5, 8): hard-rejection estimators
  (median-of-means, trimmed mean) dominate soft truncation (Catoni), which has a
  narrower stable-lr margin. Catoni looked best only because the single shared
  lr = 5e-3 sat in every method's indistinguishable linear regime.
- **Standardization and clipping are complements** (Finding 6): standardization
  fixes conditioning, clipping fixes heavy tails; both are needed.
- **Protocol fixes for the paper:** (i) never compare online learners at one
  shared lr — sweep lr per method and report best-achievable + stable-lr width;
  (ii) evaluate on a *continuous* multi-regime stream, not per-window restarts,
  which artificially penalise the clippers; (iii) measure gradient tails at a
  fixed predictor, not along a diverging trajectory.
